# The market for Skate Lodge

## What this is and where it came from

On 2026-11-20 the agent looked at every figure skating app it could find in the U.S. App Store: the seven links the owner sent, plus searches for figure skate, figure skating, ice skating practice, ice skating tracker, skate judge score and skating jump. That gave 44 apps. It profiled each one, mapped the market, studied scoring from video, drew up ideas from three angles (skater, coaches and clubs, camera) and ranked them. Reviewers then checked it, more than once: against the app descriptions, and against our own rules. The fixes are applied here, and the few not taken are listed at the end.

Everything said about another app comes from that app's App Store description as read on 2026-11-20, not from using the app. Screenshots, websites and the apps themselves were not seen. "Nobody does this" means no description says it. What we know about our own Jump Lab comes from the tests in `ROADMAP.md`.

The ranked ideas are at the end of this file, under "The ranked ideas in full". `ROADMAP.md` lists them under Suggested.

## The short answer

Skate Lodge should be the app that knows the rink. Practice logs, video numbers and coach links are now done by many small apps. What nobody has put together is the rink itself: several rinks' schedules on one page, an alert when your ice moves, how busy a session usually is, lessons tied to real sessions, and a cozy world that keeps your season. Jump Lab should win on honesty, not on claims. It should check each clip, film from one known spot, show a range, say "not measured yet" when it cannot tell, and publish how often it is right as plain counts. A coach, not the machine, gives the grade.

## The market in numbers

- 44 apps. 23 were first released in 2026, and 4 more in the second half of 2025. 9 came out since 1 October 2026.
- Almost nobody has users yet. 11 apps have no ratings, 38 of 44 have fewer than 10, and the middle app has 2.
- Skater-Stats leads with 201 ratings (average 4.96). Next come Learn To Skate USA with 30 (average 2.7), Freezio and OOFSkate with 16 each, SkateLoops with 14 and the ISU app with 12.
- Every one is an App Store app, mostly sold under one person's name, and most do one thing. Skating Storage also runs a full website.

## Video scoring today

Who measures. OOFSkate, RinkUp, Skating Storage (by hand) and Axel (undescribed), as in the table above.

Who scores. Only Skate Score claims "AI scores", called estimates, with suggested deductions for jumps, spins and step sequences. Its clips go to a cloud AI, though its Live Coaching mode runs on the phone. It has one rating, of 1 star. Every other judge-style score is typed in by a person or read from the official protocol.

Which app the owner saw is unclear. None of the seven links the owner sent describes camera scoring in its text. If it covered jumps only, OOFSkate or RinkUp fit best. If a judge-like score was the point, Skate Score fits. IceReel, one of the owner's own links, lays official judges' scores over a phone video. The scores come from the protocol, not the camera, but it may be what the owner remembered. Scribe's link pointed to a custom product page, which was not seen.

Why two phones disagree. The app sees a flat picture of the jump, and every phone flattens it differently. The causes:

1. Frames a second. At 30, takeoff and landing can each be a frame off. One of our clips went from 3.3 turns to 5.0 at half the frame rate. That clip had no known answer, so this is a likely cause, not a proven one.
2. How big the skater is. A quarter of the picture or more works. At 14 to 18%, the points drift and false jumps appear.
3. A held phone. Following the skater probably made air time too long by a few frames. No still-phone clip has been tested to compare.
4. The angle. Our turn count uses shoulder width, which works best side-on. Knee bend and lean look different from each side.
5. Blur, light and glare. Blur at takeoff added false half turns.
6. Things in the picture. A logo on the ice pulled the skeleton off the skater once.
7. Small differences tipping over a line, such as "under-rotated or not".
8. The jump really was different. Not all of the gap is camera noise.

What our tests show so far. Small skaters (14 to 18% of the picture) give loose points. A followed phone and a low frame rate probably hurt the numbers, but neither has been compared with a still 60-frame clip. On nine Commons jumps with known answers, turn counting was within a quarter turn on 2 of 9, and within half a turn on 3 of 9. All nine were triples and quads at 30 frames a second. Counting is untested at 60 frames a second and on low-level jumps. A filming mark is meant to reduce setup differences. Whether it does is untested until the owner's propped clips are run.

Our recommended approach. Keep "Measured" (what the camera sees) apart from "Graded" (a coach's call). Check each clip first. Film from one marked spot at each rink. Compare a skater's numbers only with their own clips from that spot. Putting your attempt beside the card's animation, and your best as a ghost, stay as pictures to look at. Show the middle reading with the spread of all attempts as the range. Say "can't tell, ask your coach" when a range crosses a line that matters. Check results against known answers and publish the raw counts (full approach in `ROADMAP.md`).

## Competitors worth watching

Inside Edge. The best practice log: one tap per attempt with six outcomes, Apple Watch logging, plans with targets, a free coach link, and sharpening reminders. The skater chooses which notes stay private and keeps the coach's plans and notes after unlinking. It claims over 1,000 skaters, and knows nothing about rinks. We differ by filling the log from the picked session and tying lessons to real ice.

OOFSkate. The most-rated jump measurer (16 ratings): height and rotation speed, compared with your own past jumps or a professional's. Jumps only. We differ with a clip check, ranges, a fixed filming spot and published accuracy.

Skate Score. The only app selling "AI scores", for jumps, spins and step sequences, with suggested deductions. Clips go to a cloud AI. We differ by keeping the grade with a coach and saying how sure we are.

Athlitix RinkUp. Names the widest range of jump numbers alongside OOFSkate (height, air time, rotation speed, count), keeps personal bests and trend graphs, exports video with numbers on top, and adds a social feed (follows, likes, comments). We send only the skeleton, and compare like with like.

Skating Storage. A website with an app beside it: an archive, video review with takeoff and landing marked by hand, AI answers, and skater, coach and club workspaces with Club subscriptions. We differ by leading with rinks, schedules and the world.

Frostbite. A rink hub for hockey, figure and speed skating: nearby sessions, RSVPs, a practice log with coach notes, coach connections, rink reviews, and notifications for session updates. It may already alert for moved sessions. We differ with skater-fixed schedules, crowd levels, and a map with strict sharing rules.

Open Ice. Schedules from rinks that join, ZIP search, email alerts for new matching sessions, browsing without an account. We differ by covering rinks that never join and alerting when picked sessions move.

Skater-Stats. The most-used app: competition history, scoresheets, club pages and gifted collectible tossies, some rare. We should link to it, not compete.

Axel. A skill path from first steps to quads, with animations, blade paths, tips and common mistakes, filmed attempts linked to each skill, undescribed "analysis", and hours on each pair of skates. The skater decides when a skill is ready. It is our closest rival for jump cards. We differ with measured numbers beside the card and a coach's pass.

SkatePath. 83 skills, each linked to the ones it builds on, with YouTube reference links, mastery badges, weekly goal rings and automatic streak freezes. No update since September. We differ by tying skills to measured attempts and a coach.

Clean Skate. A program planner and score calculator with GOE and components for up to nine judges, calling its scores scenario estimates. Not a rival. We keep our base value small and link out.

IceReel. Puts official judges' scores over a parent's competition video, tagged by hand. No accounts, no servers, everything on the phone. A model for privacy, not a rival.

Glide. A practice log with a social feed, kudos and typed comments, rink pages showing who trains there, private accounts and private notes. It is rated 12+. It shows the risk our rink map is built to avoid.

Scribe Skate. A training journal for sessions, lessons, tests, competitions, coach notes and insights, for skaters and families. Nothing about video. We differ with rinks, measurement and a coach link.

## The ranked ideas in full

These are the agent's suggestions, not decisions. Each says what is already Decided and what is new, and any change to a Decided item needs the owner's yes.

#### What sets Skate Lodge apart (the agent, 2026-11-20)

Twelve ideas from the market study in `MARKET.md`, ranked. All of them are Suggested: nothing here is built or decided until the owner says yes. Each one says which parts are already Decided and which are new here, and labels what it is built on as Decided, Suggested, or New (new in this list, recorded here as Suggested). What is said about other apps comes from their App Store descriptions as read on 2026-11-20. Several ideas change a Decided item or an adopted rule in `SECURITY.md`. Those changes are listed together at the end, and each needs the owner's yes.

The aim, in one line: Skate Lodge aims to make video numbers you can trust. Everyone at a rink films from one marked spot, every result is a range, we count how often results are right against known answers and publish the counts, and a coach, not the machine, gives the grade.

##### One rule for check-ins, used by every idea below

- A check-in record (which session, what time) stays on the phone, or on the server for a few hours at most. Then it becomes counts or yes-or-no flags ("checked in here today", "skated the same session"). Each flag is deleted once it has been used, for example when a report is counted or a bonus is given, so no record pairing two skaters stays.
- This rule covers check-in records, not the skater's own log. The log, with its sessions and minutes, is kept in the skater's private bundle (the Suggested data split), which no coach, admin or join can read. Only the lines a skater chooses to share go into shared tables.
- Only the passport stamp stays: the rink, the date of the first visit, and a count of visits. Only the skater sees it. A stamp for every day would be a list of past visits, so it is not kept.
- No feature lets a coach, or anyone, learn whether a skater is at a rink unless that skater approved sharing with them, person by person. Lessons a student has accepted (idea 5) are the one exception.
- The skate-together bonus never names the other skater unless the two have approved sharing both ways.

##### 1. The log that is already written

- What it is: A skater steps off the ice and most of the session is filled in: the picked session, the minutes (worked out on the phone from the check-in), and tonight's plan as a list to tick. They tap or say the results with a fixed list of outcomes: landed, fell, under-rotated, popped, two-footed, step-out. One tap picks "same plan next time" or "move the misses to the top". Skipped homework carries over. If the rink cancelled the session, the week stays whole.
- Already Decided: session reports, the kiss and cry, say it don't type it, the skate bench, coach homework and XP for it.
- New here: filling the log from the picked session and the check-in, homework carrying over, and a cancelled session not breaking the week. The six outcomes are not new: they come from Inside Edge and IceNote.
- Built on: rink schedules and the alert when a picked session moves (Decided); rink passport check-in by location (Decided); say it, don't type it (Decided); jump cards, add to tonight's plan (Decided); coach view and homework (Decided); session reports, the kiss and cry, the skate bench (Decided); rest counts (Suggested); local-first sync (Suggested); the split between private and shared data (Suggested); attempt outcomes (New, the list from Inside Edge and IceNote).
- Why it would win: Logging happens every session, so it decides whether the app gets opened. The plain log is the most crowded part of the market, so ours wins by being already done. It also feeds the skate bench's sharpening reminder, the pond and the lesson card.
- Who comes closest: Inside Edge (six outcomes, Apple Watch, plans with targets) and IceStudio (start a session from a program) are fast. Frostbite logs practices and coach notes and has RSVPs. ##### The scoring approach

- Two layers, kept apart. Measured is what the camera sees, each number with a range. Graded is a grade or a badge pass, and a coach gives it. The app never shows a machine GOE as if a judge gave it. A labelled "practice grade" range may come later as a draft for the coach.
- The clip check, before any number: 60 frames a second; the skater filling a quarter of the picture or more, as a hard gate; a still camera; the body found in 95% or more of frames; the points steady (how far a joint jumps between frames, and bone lengths staying the same); the angle; no blur at takeoff. Each measurement then shows green, yellow or red.
- Order of measurements, by evidence. First, turns per second in mid-air: the most consistent reading so far, but it has not been checked against a known answer. Before it is shown as a number it gets one, for example by counting frames by hand on the owner's propped clips. Next, air time and held positions, once the owner's propped clips are tested. Turn count stays "likely", with a range and "can't tell". Height, knee angle and lean show only from a good side-on still clip, or as rough. Spin revolutions are a reach until spins are tested. That time-based numbers change least with the camera is expected, not yet shown.
- Ranges: the median of the attempts, with the spread of all attempts. Each phone's reading is its own range. When a range crosses a line that matters (fully rotated, a badge target), the app says "can't tell, ask your coach". Accuracy is published as raw counts with the clip type ("X of Y, propped, 60 frames a second, owner's own jumps"). No rate, such as "9 times in 10", is stated until there are dozens of known answers per measurement. Real judging panels drop the highest and lowest marks (from memory, not checked). The app does not claim to copy that rule until it is checked.
- The numbers compare a skater only with their own clips from the same spot. Putting your attempt beside the card's animation, and your best as a ghost, stay as pictures to look at.
- Edges, turns and footwork: "not measured yet, ask your coach", with the research item in idea 4.
- Nothing that depends on turn count is shown as one number until the owner's propped 60-frame clips are tested. The professor asked for those results by the end of the week of 2026-11-23. Today, on Commons triples and quads at 30 frames a second, the counter is within a quarter turn on 2 of 9 known jumps, and within half a turn on 3 of 9. It is untested at 60 frames a second and on low-level jumps.

## Changes these ideas would make to Decided items

- Jump cards (Decided: a card turns gold on the first landing and becomes the milestone card): four steps to gold. Tried, Landed (the milestone card, now frost silver), Steady, Coach-passed (gold). A pass tied to a scheduled lesson and confirmed by the skater can carry 'seen on the ice'. The card never names the coach, to friends, visitors or anyone else. Idea 4.
- Cue cards (Decided: the coach taps cues 'from a list or adds their own') and coach homework (Decided: 'assign homework'): limiting cues to the list only would remove the coach's own words. The recommended version keeps cue cards as Decided and adds a short length limit, a history the club admin can see, and report and block on the coach. Homework becomes a pick of skill cards plus a count or target from a list, and coach corrections become a mark picked from a list. Any own words in cues, homework or corrections get the same treatment as cue cards. Either way, the SafeSport rule on messages between adults and young athletes is checked first; (from memory, not checked) it may expect such messages to be visible to a parent, and parent accounts are Set aside. Idea 6.
- Coach view (Decided: 'see students' progress'): the coach sees only the lines the skater turns on (minutes, outcomes, homework ticked, Jump Lab ranges 'compared with your clips from the same spot'). Lines arrive the next day with the day of the week only, or as weekly totals, and weekly totals are the default for a skater under 18. Off until the skater turns each line on, and never rink, start time, session or video. Ideas 1 and 6.
- Lessons and SECURITY.md section 1 (adopted rule: nobody's past visits are ever listed for anyone else) and the proposed check-in rule: lessons are the one exception to 'never rink, start time or session'. A coach sends a lesson series as a request the student accepts or turns down. Only future accepted lessons are kept with their rink and time, seen by that coach and that student only. Once a lesson has passed, its rink and time are deleted and only a count stays. Coach accounts need two-step sign-in, and lesson tables join the section 3 access tests. Idea 5.
- The alert when a picked session moves, Plan my week, and the coach board (all Decided): widened so accepted lessons sit on real sessions, the alert carries the coach's choice to move or cancel lessons, Plan my week takes coach homework as targets on real sessions, and the coach board's open times become places on the schedule. Idea 5.
- Session reports (Decided: one tap each for busy and ice when a session is saved): a report counts toward 'usually quiet' only with a 'checked in here today' flag at that rink, and the flag is deleted once the report is counted. Idea 7.
- Jump card animations (Decided: poses from a clean jump by a skater who agrees, skeleton only): add that no card animation is taken from a skater under 18. Idea 9.
- Jump Lab (Decided: measure air time, height, rotations, rotation speed, landing, axis): results are shown as ranges with 'can't tell, ask your coach'. Turn count is not shown as one number until the owner's propped 60-frame clips are tested, and rotation speed is not shown as a number until it is checked against a known answer. Height, knee angle and lean show only from a good side-on still clip, or as rough. Measurements are brought in by evidence, and spins are a reach. The numbers compare only same-spot clips; the side-by-side with the card's animation and your best as a ghost stay as pictures. Scoring approach.

Voice is tested on a real iPhone and checked against Apple's documentation before it is planned. Recorded audio is never sent to our server or the AI. The owner picks: speech recognition that runs on the phone, an exception, or tapping only. Idea 1.

The skate-together bonus never names the other skater unless the two approved sharing both ways. The check-in rule.
